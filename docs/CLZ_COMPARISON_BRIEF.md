## The five approved talking points (refined with verified data)

### 1. Design & feel
**Lichtenstein/pop-art aesthetic, modern PWA, mobile-first.**

CLZ feels like 2010 software updated to run on 2026 phones. Their app shell, navigation, and visual language all carry the legacy of being a desktop catalog tool ported to mobile. **Collectors Chest was designed for the phone first** - gradient headers, comic-book typography, swipeable lists, full-screen lightbox for cover verification. It feels like an app a comic collector built for comic collectors, not a database tool for accountants.

> **Floor pitch:** *"Open both apps side-by-side. Within 5 seconds, people see the difference."*

### 2. Cost (the apples-to-apples reframe)
**Roughly half the price for the same outcome.**

This is the line that needs the most careful delivery because CLZ's headline price ($1.99/mo for the mobile app) is *cheaper* than ours. **The trick is reframing to "what it costs to actually use it":**

- CLZ Mobile alone is just a catalog - no real-time pricing
- To see actual comic values, you need CovrPrice Premium **on top** at $8.95/mo
- That's $10.94/mo total to track + value
- Collectors Chest Premium is $4.99/mo, **pricing included**

> **Floor pitch:** *"They're cheaper if you only want a list of titles. The second you want to know what your books are actually worth, you're paying CovrPrice on top - and that's where they get more expensive than us."*

> **Avoid this mistake:** Don't say "we're cheaper than CLZ" without context. A savvy collector will check $1.99 vs $4.99 and walk away. Lead with the apples-to-apples reframe.

### 3. Authenticity (your books, not stock images)
**Your real cover photo. Their stock catalog image.**

CLZ shows you a manufactured/canonical cover image for each comic in your collection. Collectors Chest shows you **the photo you took of YOUR actual book.** This is more than a UX thing - it's an emotional thing. Your collection looks like *your* collection. The wear on the cover, the tape repair on the corner, the slight dog-ear on page 3 - they're all there. Your collection becomes a digital twin of your physical shelf, not an inventory list.

> **Floor pitch:** *"Pull up someone's collection on each app. You see their actual books in ours. You see Marvel's marketing-department cover in CLZ's. Which feels more like YOUR collection?"*

> **Why this matters for selling:** When sellers list books for sale on our marketplace, buyers see the *actual condition photo* by default - not a generic stock image. That alone reduces dispute risk versus eBay-style listings.

### 4. Free tier + extended trial
**5 free guest scans + 10/month free tier + 30-day Premium trial. CLZ has none of those - 7-day trial only.**

This is a conversion-funnel argument:
- A skeptical collector at a con can pull out their phone, scan a book in our app, and see real eBay-priced value **in 5 seconds, with zero commitment** - no account, no card, no app store subscription dialog
- The same collector can't do that with CLZ - they have to commit to at least starting the trial (which usually means an Apple/Google subscription dialog)
- Even after they create an account, our free tier gets them 10 scans/month forever - CLZ goes to $1.99/mo (or $10.94/mo with pricing) on day 8

> **Floor pitch:** *"Take out your phone right now. I'll show you 5 scans, no signup. You decide if you want to keep going. CLZ won't let you do that."*

### 5. No-account scanning
**Guest scans. CLZ requires a subscription commitment from second 1.**

You can scan a comic on Collectors Chest **without ever creating an account** - try the product, build trust, then sign up. CLZ requires the trial subscription dialog before any meaningful use of the app. **The friction at "you must commit before you can taste" kills first-show conversions.**

> **Floor pitch:** *"How often have you downloaded an app, hit a paywall on launch, and just deleted it? Most people just delete. We don't make you commit until you've already gotten value out of us."*

## Common objections (with responses)

### *"CLZ has 99% barcode scan accuracy, you don't have that yet."*
**Acknowledge → reframe.**
> *"True - they've spent 15+ years on a barcode database, and we're not there yet on barcode-only. But we don't need to be: our cover-recognition AI scans the FRONT of the book, which means we work on every comic ever printed - including the silver/bronze keys CLZ's barcode lookup misses entirely because barcodes didn't exist before 1973. If you're scanning new books only, CLZ wins. If you're scanning a vintage collection, we win."*

### *"I already paid for CLZ. Why switch?"*
**Don't push the switch. Push the side-by-side.**
> *"Don't switch - try us alongside it. Our free tier gives you 10 scans a month at no cost. Test our cover recognition on a book CLZ misses. If we save you time, the switch will make sense on its own. If not, you've lost nothing."*

### *"I need a desktop app, not just mobile."*
**Be honest.**
> *"We're web-first - runs on any browser, including desktop Chrome. We don't have a native macOS/Windows app today. CLZ has that and it's their best feature for power users. We're betting most collectors will catalog from the phone they already have in their pocket. If you live at a desk, CLZ's desktop tool may be a better fit right now - but we're getting to native apps soon."*

### *"Aren't you new? What if you go out of business?"*
**Show the moat.**
> *"Fair question. Two answers: (1) CSV export in three clicks - your data is portable, never locked in. (2) We're not a side project: live since [DATE], private beta growing, full marketplace shipped, real revenue. We're not going anywhere - but if we did, your collection data leaves with you in the same format you'd import to any competitor."*

### *"Why don't you have CGC slab pricing?"*
**Yes we do - and accurately.**
> *"We do - graded books are auto-detected by our scanner reading the slab label, and our pricing engine pulls grade-aware comps from eBay's actual sales data. CLZ's CovrPrice add-on does graded values too, but you're paying $8.95/mo extra for it. Ours is included in the $4.99 Premium."*

### *"Does it work offline at conventions?"*
**Yes - and that's a feature, not a bug.**
> *"Key Hunt mode works offline. Once you've scanned a book, the result caches locally, so even when convention WiFi dies you can re-check prices instantly. CLZ's cloud sync means they need a connection. We're built for the convention floor."*

### Strategic read

**Where we should consider closing gaps (in priority order):**

1. **Storage box location field** - trivial schema change; real collector value. Single text field. *Could ship in 30 minutes.*
2. **Back cover photo upload** - easy add; meaningful for slab listings + graded books. *~2 hours.*
3. **Signature / autograph tracking** - boolean + text field. Low-effort. *~1 hour.*
4. **Improve barcode scanning speed** - our biggest functional gap vs CLZ. BACKLOG already has it. *Multi-day project.*
5. **Custom fields** - defer until requested.
6. **Native desktop app** - defer; web works.

**Where we should NOT try to compete:**

- Don't try to out-database CLZ on barcode coverage. They've had 15 years; we'd lose that race for years. **Compete on cover-AI for vintage instead** - that's a market they can't enter without rebuilding their stack.
- Don't try to mimic their desktop app. Our PWA is a feature, not a limitation - it works everywhere without a download.

**Where we have moats they can't quickly replicate:**

- **Marketplace** - they'd need 12-18 months and Stripe Connect compliance work to ship something equivalent.
- **AI cover recognition** - requires retraining their stack; not just a feature toggle.
- **Modern PWA / design language** - not a feature you can bolt on; it's the foundation.
- **Pricing transparency** - our $4.99 all-in vs their $1.99+$8.95 stack is structural, not a knob they can turn.

### What this means for the convention floor pitch

**Lead with the moats. Acknowledge their strengths honestly. Skip the gaps.**

> *"CLZ has been around forever and they're great if you only collect modern books with barcodes - their barcode database is the best out there. But the second you want to scan a Silver Age key, sell something out of your collection, or see what your books are worth without paying a separate subscription for that data - that's where we win. We're built for the collector who actually sells, trades, and chases vintage keys."*

Don't shy away from CLZ's strengths - owning them in conversation makes everything else more credible.

## Data partnerships & industry context (as of May 6, 2026)

> **Floor framing:** This came up repeatedly at the May 3-4 show. Customers want to know where our pricing data comes from and whether we're a flash-in-the-pan or built to last. The honest answer here is a real differentiator - competitors are MORE exposed to the industry data shake-up than we are.

### What we're doing - actively

**CovrPrice - early in their partner pipeline (API targeting 2027)**
Reached out about API / B2B partnership. CovrPrice's Director of Operations confirmed (May 6, 2026) that they do not currently offer a public API but are **targeting 2027 to begin a public API offering**. They aren't taking calls on the feature yet, but invited input on what the API should support. We've replied requesting beta-tester status when it launches and surfaced our users' #1 ask: accurate sales pricing data. Status: queued / on their radar; nothing signed and nothing imminent. CovrPrice is the same data CLZ users pay `+$8.95/mo` for - landing this post-2027 would let us include it in the $4.99 Premium tier.

**CovrPrice - key issue / first-appearance details (same pipeline)**
The same future API would expose CovrPrice's 15+ years of curated key-issue and first-appearance data. Layering that on top of our 1,130-entry hand-curated DB would meaningfully expand niche/variant coverage. Same 2027 timing - no separate track.

### What's happening industry-wide (context, not gossip)

**GoCollect API - shut down (Feb 2026)**
GoCollect ran a Pro-tier API for years (100 calls/day) that competitors used for FMV pricing, Hot 50 lists, and trend data. They closed the program in February 2026 - not granting access to new partners, including us. This affects every comic-tracker app that depended on their data.

**Marvel Developer API - deprecated (Feb 2026)**
Marvel's developer program (free tier, 3K calls/day for cover art + metadata) was officially deprecated in the same window. Apps that used Marvel's API for catalog data are now scrambling for alternatives.

### Why this matters for our positioning

Competitors built on third-party API stacks are exposed to industry consolidation. We saw this coming and **built our own data spine**:
- **AI cover recognition** - our own pipeline, not a Marvel API wrapper
- **eBay Browse API** for live pricing - already integrated, working today
- **1,130-entry curated key DB** - our own, in source code, no API dependency
- **Hand-grown community covers + ZenRows fallback** - diversified cover sources

**The resilience pitch:**
> *"The whole comics-data industry is consolidating right now. Marvel pulled their API. GoCollect shut their API program down. CLZ depends entirely on their CovrPrice partnership - that's currently the only one of those still working for them. CovrPrice told us their public API is on their 2027 roadmap, and we've been positioned as an early beta tester for when it launches. But the bigger story is we built our own AI scanning, pricing, and curated key-info pipeline from day one. We're not dependent on any one data source. That's why we'll still be here in five years."*

### What NOT to say

- ❌ Don't say "active conversations" or "in discussions" with CovrPrice. The honest line is "CovrPrice's public API is on their 2027 roadmap; we're queued as an early beta tester."
- ❌ Don't claim a CovrPrice deal is signed or imminent. **It is not.** No public API exists yet.
- ❌ Don't disparage GoCollect. They made a business decision; we don't know their reasoning. State the facts neutrally - they discontinued their API program.
- ❌ Don't promise specific timelines for CovrPrice integration beyond "their 2027 target." Even after the API launches, integration is its own engineering effort.
- ❌ Don't share email contents, contact names, or any specifics of partner correspondence. The customer doesn't need them; the team doesn't want them public.

### What's safe to say at the booth

- ✅ "CovrPrice told us their public API is on their 2027 roadmap, and we've been positioned as an early beta tester for when it launches."
- ✅ "GoCollect closed their API program in February - that affects everyone in this space."
- ✅ "We built our own AI scanning and pricing pipeline so we're not dependent on any single data provider."
- ✅ "Whatever happens with future data partnerships, your scans still work, your collection still syncs, and your data is still exportable."
